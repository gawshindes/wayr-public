# Importance criteria — Example Digest

## Who reads this
People who work in or around software and want the day's developments in five minutes, without reading twenty sites.

## What matters most (score high)
- Launches, releases and pricing changes from the companies whose products readers use
- Security incidents with named victims, or a fix people should apply today
- Regulation, rulings and policy that change what companies or users can do
- Deals at scale: acquisitions, funding above $100M, IPOs, shutdowns, large layoffs

## What matters less (score low)
- Incremental versions and minor features; earnings without a strategic change
- Rumours and unsourced reports; opinion and prediction pieces
- Gadget reviews, deals and buying guides

## Calibration
Use the whole scale. A typical issue has one or two stories at 0.8 or above and most
at 0.3–0.5; if everything lands at 0.5 the score is not doing its job.
- 0.9–1.0: the story every reader will be asked about.
- 0.7–0.8: clearly among the most consequential for these readers.
- 0.5–0.6: worth a line in the roundup.
- 0.2–0.4: real but minor, or for a different reader.
- 0.0–0.1: off-topic, opinion, or a repeat.
